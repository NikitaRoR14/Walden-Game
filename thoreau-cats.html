Walden Footnotes

Thoreau's Cats

Thoreau never kept a house cat at Walden. These notes gather what he actually wrote about farm cats, strays, and their place in Concord’s ecology.

🏠

No resident cabin cat

Walden, 1845–1847

Thoreau’s cabin was spare by design. He wrote about mice, loons, foxes, and owls-but no cat lived with him there.

🐭

Concord mousers

Common farm cats

Mid-19th century New England households kept cats mainly as mousers. Thoreau mentions “the common cat” doing practical work around town homes.

🐦

Predation notes

Journal observations

Thoreau recorded that domestic cats hunted birds and small wildlife-an early note on their impact. He contrasts “tame cats” with the wildness he valued.

🐾

Tracks in the woods

Far from any house

In his journals, Thoreau notes seeing a cat deep in the woods and remarks on how far it was from any farmhouse-an example of domestic animals straying into wild places.

Thoreau’s Field Notes

  • He never lists a cat among his Walden companions (he does list mice, loons, owls, foxes).
  • He refers to “common cats” as mousers in Concord homes-useful, but not wild.
  • He notes domestic cats hunting birds, an early observation on their effect on local wildlife.
  • He records finding a cat in the woods far from houses, underscoring his theme of wildness versus domestic life.
